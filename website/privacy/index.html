Privacy

Explain the boundary clearly.

Amon’s public site should be direct about what the product is designed to do, what it does not claim, and where the important lines are between transient service behavior, protected sessions, and local workspace storage.

Current posture

Private by default is a product direction, not a magic phrase.

  • Amon is being built to minimize durable storage of query text, result sets, and page content on the service side.
  • Saved work is intended to live in a local workspace by default.
  • Protected Session exists for selected public-web tasks that need the live site.
  • The product aims to reduce unnecessary persistence and exposure, not to pretend the open web disappears.
Service reality

What still exists operationally

  • Authentication, entitlement, session, and rate-limit metadata still exist as part of operating a service.
  • Public websites you choose to visit directly still have their own logs, policies, and visibility.
  • A public site should never imply that internal admin or ops tools are part of the user-facing surface.
  • The right trust posture is honest minimization plus clear product boundaries.
What we can say today

Amon is narrower and more concrete than a generic “private browser” claim.

The site should talk about the actual v1 direction: public-web search and retrieval, clean reading, selected protected sessions, and local workspace storage. That is specific enough to be credible and broad enough to matter.

What we should not say

No sweeping promises that outrun the product.

Avoid claiming perfect anonymity, invisible browsing, universal protection, or finished functionality that is still in progress. The strongest trust signal is precise language matched to the real architecture.

Amon. Honest privacy posture over privacy theater.
